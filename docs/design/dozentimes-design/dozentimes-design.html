AThe answer

dozentimes is two small time tools that share one contract, and on the synthetic test the pair shows every fact with the right status and nothing false as true, which neither tool does alone.

dozentimes-intf reads which time a question is about: now, a past date, a span, the future, or a past date plus what changed since. dozentimes-agnt keeps dated facts from project docs, knows when each one goes stale, and re-reads the source before it shows a stale fact as current. pi-time is pi launched with both tools in its own agent directory, over a ledger built from project docs.

The 1+1=3 test runs seven questions under four setups: no tools, intent only, ledger only, and both. Status accuracy adds up: from a 36% baseline, intent alone gains 43 points, the ledger alone 21, and both together 64, so the interaction term is 0.00. Harm falls by more than the sum: intent alone removes 2 of the 4 false-as-true facts, the ledger alone removes none, and both together remove all 4, an interaction of -2. With a fresh ledger per question the pair spends 9 source re-reads where the ledger alone spends 24. The fixtures are synthetic, so this shows the mechanism and not a rate on real data. On 21 contrived questions Jev reads 9 of the 10 traps that fool the rules, where the rules read none, so Jev's value on real questions still waits on the blind set in #1.

A fresh session works the open issues in the order in Section 08, starting with #12, gated by the suites, and #1 waits on about 50 blind questions from you.

01Architecture01 / 10

A question passes through the four parts in one direction. dozentimes-core holds the contract and the plumbing both tools share: the types, the SQLite schema, the trace writer, the clock and the Jev client. dozentimes-intf turns a prompt plus conversation state into a TemporalIntent. dozentimes-agnt reads the ledger at the time that intent names and re-checks stale facts against their source files. pi-time wires both into pi through one extension: a hook reads every prompt, and four tools expose the pair to the model.

Jev sits inside dozentimes-intf as an optional judge, and a rule-based fallback answers whenever Jev is off or fails. Every parse, ingest, read and maintenance pass writes a row to the traces table inside the same ledger file.

02dozentimes-core: the contract02 / 10

The contract is two records. intf produces a TemporalIntent, agnt consumes it and returns LedgerFacts, and dates are ISO calendar dates with no time zone.

The intent carries seven fields.

Statuses and shelf lives

StatusMeaning
currentStill true as far as the source shows.
replacedA newer line or a re-read closed it. Never shown as current.
needs_checkPast its shelf life and not confirmed by a re-read.
current_thenTrue at the past date or span the question asks about.

Shelf lives are hand-set per class: status 7 days, decision 90, preference 365, identity never. A ponytail comment in the contract marks them for fitting from verification outcomes.

SQLite schema

The SQLite schema has five tables in one file, opened in WAL mode at ~/.dozentimes/ledger.db unless DOZENTIMES_DB names another.

The schema is five tables.

TableHolds
factsOne row per fact, unique on source path, section and text.
facts_ftsFTS5 index over project, section and text, filled by an insert trigger.
checksOne row per re-read: the fact, its age in days, whether it changed, and why.
sourcesOne row per source file: its hash and when it was last ingested.
tracesOne row per tool call: tool, input, output, path, milliseconds and any error.

Traces, clock and the Jev client

Traces clip each input and output to 2,000 characters, and the newest rows feed /dozentimes and the trace command line. DOZENTIMES_NOW pins the clock, which is how tests, the replay and the end-to-end suite set dates.

The Jev client is one fetch to the TypeSafe System One endpoint, with the model pinned to jev-1.13.0 unless DOZENTIMES_JEV_MODEL says otherwise. It is on only when TYPESAFE_API_KEY is set and DOZENTIMES_JEV is not off, and it throws on any failure instead of guessing, because every caller has a fallback. Jev answers two question types: a choice among labels with a confidence, and a yes-or-no probability.

03dozentimes-intf: temporal intent03 / 10

Candidates in code, judgments by Jev

Code finds the candidates and does every date sum, and Jev only picks labels. chrono-node finds date phrases against the session clock. Code then turns "last week" or "next month" into Monday-to-Sunday weeks or whole months, widens a bare month to its full span, and merges "between X and Y" into one range.

The rules pick the mode from the candidate and a few word lists. A stated date in the future, or a future word, reads as future. "Since" or "what changed" reads as mixed. A span reads as range, and a single date as past. With no date, a now word reads as current, then a held as-of is inherited, then a future word reads as future. Anything else is none.

When Jev is on, one batched call asks up to four questions: which mode, whether the question is about now, which candidate phrase sets the time, and, when an as-of is held, whether this question changes the period. A choice counts only at confidence 0.5 or more, and a yes only at probability 0.5 or more. Jev never writes a date. It picks one of the candidates code found, or none. On any Jev error the rule reading stands and the error is stored on the intent and in the trace row.

Ambient as-of

The ambient as-of is the period a conversation is in. A stated past date, span or since-question sets it. A follow-up with no date inherits it. A new stated period replaces it. A current reading clears it: on the rules that takes "now", "currently" or "today", and on the Jev path Jev's own now judgment. A Jev yes to "does this change the period" also clears it, and /dozentimes asof sets or clears it by hand.

Guards and Jev's now judgment

Live Jev failures shaped how Jev and the rules combine. An inherited reading is kept: Jev's mode choice alone cannot drop it, and only the "does this change the period" judgment can. Live Jev had read the follow-up "And the next step for alpha?" as future, because of the word "next". A second guard once let an explicit now word override Jev's mode, after live Jev read "What is the next step for alpha now?" as future. The probe then showed that guard threw away three of Jev's right answers, such as "Was that still true at the time?", which has "still" and is about the past. Since commit e9ef5f7, on the Jev path "is this about right now?" is Jev's own judgment, and the keywords decide only on the fallback path. Jev still reads "next step" as future at 0.9 confidence while its own now judgment says now at 0.8, and the combination follows the now judgment.

04dozentimes-agnt: the freshness ledger04 / 10

Sources, extraction and dating

Sources are whats_next.md, HANDOFF.md, docs/handoffs/*.md and learning_guide*.html under ~/create and ~/repos, at most four folders deep, skipping dot folders and node_modules. DOZENTIMES_ROOTS replaces the two roots.

Markdown extraction takes each bullet or checkbox line of 12 characters or more outside code fences, under its nearest heading. Learning guides give one fact per definition term and per first table cell, under their section heading. A fact's project is the folder that holds its file, or the repo root for docs/handoffs.

Dating follows the nearest date the author wrote. In markdown that is the nearest dated heading, then a date prefix on the filename, then the file's modified date. In a learning guide it is the section heading's date, then the last date in the title, then the modified date. At ingest, last_verified_at is the author's date and verified_by is author, because reading a line is not checking it. Jev classifies each new line into a volatility class in batches of 40, and a keyword rule takes over on error.

Replacement rules

Four replacement rules close a fact at ingest.

The rules carry these letters in the code.

RuleClosesWhenClosed at
(a)An open checkboxThe same text appears checked in the same file.The checked line's date
(b)A Next: lineIts successor in the project: the next later-dated one, or the next one lower in the same file on the same day.The successor's date
(c)Any lineIt is gone from its file on re-read.The day it was found gone
(d)A state-like section of a handoffA later-dated handoff of the same project has the same section (State, Next step, Recent artifacts and similar).The later handoff's date

A removed line that comes back is reopened, and the gap between removal and return is not kept.

Verification, reads and maintain

Verification is a re-read of the source file. If the file's hash changed since the last ingest, the file is re-ingested first so the replacement rules run. The fact is verified when its line is still there and no rule closed it: last_verified_at moves to today and verified_by to source. Otherwise it is closed today. Every re-read writes a checks row with the fact's age and whether it changed, which is the data shelf-life fitting needs.

Each read takes a text-match pool five times the limit, then prefers live facts and newer ones. The status depends on the mode.

Three read shapes cover the six modes.

ModeFacts returnedStatusRe-reads
current, none, futureAll matchesreplaced if closed, needs_check if past shelf life, else currentneeds_check facts
past, rangeValid on or before the anchor's end and not closed before its startcurrent_thenNone
mixedThe past set at the anchor, plus live facts dated after itcurrent_then for the past set, current rules for the restThe newer facts only

When a re-read catches a change, the read runs once more without re-reading, so facts the re-ingest added can answer. Maintain re-checks stale live facts up to a budget, most recently read first, then the longest unconfirmed.

05pi-time05 / 10

Launcher and agent directory

The launcher sets PI_CODING_AGENT_DIR to pi-time/agent, so pi-time keeps its own settings, sessions and extension apart from the user's pi. It links auth.json and fd from ~/.pi/agent instead of copying them, installs chrono-node if it is missing, and starts pi with --no-skills so shared skills stay out. The agent settings pin provider zai, model glm-5.3 and project trust never.

Tools, per-turn reading and status line

Four tools reach the model.

ToolDoes
dozentimes_askRuns the pair: the turn's reading, the ledger read and the re-checks, a status per fact.
temporal_intentReads the time reference only, and updates the held as-of like a turn.
ledger_queryReads the ledger with an explicit mode and anchor, to override the reading.
ledger_verifyRe-reads one fact's source and returns its status before and after.

Before each turn a hook parses the prompt, adds the reading and three rules to the system prompt, and updates the status line. The rules are to call dozentimes_ask for project questions, say the reading back in one line, and never present a replaced or needs_check fact as current. The status line reads "dt <facts> facts | <stale> stale | reading: as of 2026-10-14 (inherited from earlier) | jev ok", and the last field turns to "jev FALLBACK n" after any Jev error in the session. /dozentimes shows counts by status and class, the held as-of, the top projects, the last 10 traces and the remaining work. Its subcommands are ingest, maintain, asof and trace.

Startup and remaining work

On startup pi-time builds the ledger if it is empty, or re-ingests only the files whose hash changed, then lists remaining work. The list is the open GitHub issues, needs-input first, then agent:todo, then a count of ideas, with REMAINING.md as the offline fallback.

Observability and privacy

Every Jev failure is loud. The trace row keeps the error, the status line counts fallbacks, /dozentimes trace marks the row ERROR, and the answer still arrives on the rules. A test hook, DOZENTIMES_TRACE_FILE, writes each turn's reading and each dozentimes_ask result as one JSON line. Without a terminal UI the extension also writes to stderr, and inside the UI it never does, because stderr lines tear the pi screen.

The repo is public. The ledger lives outside it, and sessions, the auth link, the fd link, model store and trust file are gitignored. Fixtures are synthetic, replay reports hold counts and rates only, and issue bodies name engineering work, not corpus content.

On the real corpus

On today's corpus a build with keyword classes finds 70 source files, 13 of them learning guides, and 1,914 facts, of which 49 are replaced and 278 are past their shelf life. Keyword classes label most lines decision, so a build with Jev classes, as pi-time runs it, finds more status facts and more stale ones.

Harm is the count of harmful facts shown as current or current_then, so the interaction 0 - 2 - 4 + 4 = -2 means the pair removes 2 more than the two tools remove separately. The ledger alone removes no harm because it re-checks, finds the newest fact, and then shows it as true for questions about the past. Intent score, replay and end to end

The intent score is a regression check: the rules get 20 of 20 and Jev 19 of 20 on asof_mode, both 20 of 20 on anchor and basis, at 2 ms and 112 ms per question. The labels were written next to the rules, so it does not measure accuracy.

The replay on real history scores the ledger against git, not against its own rules. Over 50 sources and 143 sample dates, recall against git truth is 0.998 and precision is 1.0 once 345 backdated lines are set aside. 22 lines were misdated. The freshness check over 479 version pairs shows the 7-day status shelf life flags 4,672 facts of which 14% changed, about 7 flagged for each real change, and it catches 61% of status changes. The share of status facts changed rises with age: 7% at 0 to 7 days, 12% at 8 to 30, and 35% at 31 to 90. No fact in the sample is older than 90 days, so the decision and preference shelf lives never fire.

The end-to-end suite drives real pi with glm-5.3 through the seven questions as one conversation and checks the tool call, the reading, the facts and three Jev-judged checks per turn. The latest run passed 14 of 14 turns over 2 runs at about $0.06 per run as pi reports cost. With a bad Jev key all 7 turns still answered, and 8 of 8 trace rows recorded the error.

The Jev probe scores the rules alone, Jev's raw mode choice alone, and the combination on 21 contrived questions. On the 10 traps worded to fool the keyword rules, the rules get 0, Jev alone 9 and the combination 9. All three get the 6 controls right. On the 3 questions with no time in them the rules get 3 and Jev 2, because Jev read "What color is the alpha logo?" as current. On the 2 questions about a past with no date, such as "What did we believe before the launch?", Jev gets both and the combination neither, because the contract has no way to say "past" without an anchor date. That gap is filed as #13.

10Risks and limits10 / 10

  • The ablation and end-to-end suites run on synthetic fixtures: seven questions over three projects, two edits behind the ledger. They show the mechanism, not a rate.
  • The 20 intent labels were written next to the rules, and the ablation gold was written with its fixtures for this test, so passing either is a regression check.
  • The replay samples are thin: 50 sources, 143 sample dates and 479 version pairs, correlated within each source, with no confidence intervals and no fact older than 90 days.
  • The replay's rule (d) over-reach check is inconclusive: it flags all 1,842 cases, because the filename test passes whenever two handoffs have different names and cannot tell a new unit of work from the same unit renamed.
  • Two end-to-end runs do not give a stable pass rate, and the suite's own notes name three sources of judge and model noise.
  • The fallback end-to-end run predates commit e9ef5f7, which also reordered the rules so that a held as-of now wins over a future word.
  • The 7-day status shelf life is costly on real history: it flags 4,672 facts of which 14% changed, about 7 re-reads for each change it finds.
  • Project text leaves the machine: ingest sends each new line to Jev, TypeSafe's hosted API, in batches of 40 for classification, and pi-time sends questions and ledger facts to the zai model. DOZENTIMES_JEV=off keeps classification and intent reading on the local rules, and the model calls remain.
  • The probe is contrived against the rules by design, so its scores show that Jev is not echoing the rules and say nothing about accuracy.
